# Repair: Content Map

The source of truth for **what** the app teaches. Each module's authored content lives in
`content/modules/<slug>/` and is validated by `pnpm content:build`. Modules marked
`draft` in their `module.yaml` have an outline only; their titles here are the plan.

**Status:** Module 1 (*Calming the Storm*) is fully authored (`in_review`, pending
clinical sign-off). Modules 2–14 are outlined (`draft`). The global onboarding check,
private safety screen, check-ins, weekly *State of Us* and crisis resources are authored.

### Research basis for module selection
- **Why couples seek help.** Doss, Simpson & Christensen (2004) found emotional affection and communication tied for first, then divorce concerns, arguments, children, sex, personal problems and trust. The BACP/Relate study (UK) found communication 79.7%, arguments 68.9%, conflict management 67.8%, and fear the relationship will end 65.1%. Couples wait about 6 years after problems begin before getting help, so users arrive in an entrenched state. That makes de-escalation the first module and a "crossroads" (stay or go) module necessary.
- **Evidence-based frameworks to draw on, in original wording.** The Gottman Method covers conflict patterns, repair, flooding, turning toward, and the atone/attune/attach approach to affair recovery. EFT (Emotionally Focused Therapy) covers attachment, the "negative cycle" and softening. IBCT (Integrative Behavioral Couple Therapy) covers acceptance and the unified detachment stance. Doherty's discernment counseling covers the stay-or-go decision. OurRelationship (Doss) is an online program shown in RCTs to work for distressed couples. Its format of observe, understand, respond is a useful model for each module's arc.
- **Safety research on IPV apps (JMIR 2021).** Abuse screening must use validated items and cover emotional violence and coercive control, not only physical violence, and it must be private to each partner. Couples exercises are contraindicated where there is coercive control. Safety is therefore a system that runs through the whole app, not a module.

## Part 1: Activity types (the shared "player" engine)
Every module is assembled from the same building blocks:

| Type | Description | Solo / Together |
|---|---|---|
| **Assessment** | 8–15 Likert or multiple-choice items with a scored profile, a result narrative and recommended activities. Can be retaken to show change over time. | Each partner takes it alone; results are compared side by side only if both opt in |
| **Lesson** | 3–6 swipeable cards (text, illustration, optional audio), about 3–5 min, ending in a one-question "takeaway" check | Solo |
| **Exercise** | A guided step-by-step task: worksheets, lists, rankings, timers | Solo or together |
| **Conversation Guide** | Structured speaker/listener turns with a timer, prompts on screen, a mid-conversation "pause/flooding" button, and a closing appreciation | Together, on one phone or both phones synced in realtime |
| **Reflection / Journal** | Prompted writing. **Private by default**; sharing is explicit, per entry | Solo |
| **Check-in** | A 30-second daily mood and connection pulse, plus a weekly 10-minute "State of Us" conversation | Both |
| **Ritual / Challenge** | A 7- or 14-day micro-habit with daily nudges | Both |

Each module follows the same arc: **Assess → Understand (lessons) → Practice (exercises) → Talk (conversation guides) → Reflect → Re-assess**. A module has about 1 assessment, 5 lessons, 5 exercises, 3 conversation guides, 1 journal set and 1 ritual.

## Part 2: Module library (14 modules)

### Tier A: Start here (stabilize)
**1. Calming the Storm (De-escalation & Safety First)**: the default first module for high-distress couples
- Assessment: *Conflict Temperature*. Covers frequency and intensity of fights, flooding, time to recover, and fear of the relationship ending.
- Lessons: Your nervous system in a fight (flooding, heart rate around 100 bpm) · The negative cycle: it's you two vs. the pattern · Why "winning" loses · Taking a break the right way · A ceasefire is not avoidance
- Exercises: Map our cycle (pursue/withdraw diagram builder) · Personal flooding signals · Self-soothing toolkit (breathing timer, grounding) · Write a time-out agreement · List of "topics on hold"
- Conversation guides: Agreeing on a time-out signal · Naming our cycle together · 10-minute low-stakes reconnection
- Ritual: 7-day ceasefire (no hot topics, one daily appreciation)

**2. Communicating Without Wounding**
- Assessment: *Communication Patterns* (criticism, defensiveness, contempt, stonewalling, listening quality)
- Lessons: Complaints vs. criticism · The gentle start-up formula (feeling + situation + need) · Listening to understand · Validation isn't agreement · Contempt and its antidote · Noticing defensiveness
- Exercises: Rewrite the criticism · Feelings and needs vocabulary builder · Turn a "you always" into an "I need" · Practicing reflective listening
- Conversation guides: Speaker-listener drill (low-stakes topic) · "What I meant vs. what you heard" · Weekly State of Us
- Ritual: 14-day "one appreciation, one curiosity question"

**3. Fighting Fair (Managing Conflict)**
- Assessment: *Conflict Style Profile* (avoider, volatile, validating, hostile) plus solvable vs. perpetual issues
- Lessons: Solvable vs. perpetual problems · Repair attempts: the most important skill · Compromise without losing yourself · The dreams hidden inside gridlock · Accepting influence
- Exercises: Sort our issues (solvable/perpetual) · Repair phrase card deck (pick favorites) · Core needs vs. flexible areas (concentric circles)
- Conversation guides: Solving a solvable problem (5 steps) · Dreams within conflict (for gridlocked issues) · Processing a past fight (aftermath debrief: feelings → perceptions → triggers → ownership → plan)

### Tier B: Repair
**4. Repairing Trust**: everyday betrayals such as broken promises, lies, secrecy, and turning away in moments of need
- Assessment: *Trust Inventory* (reliability, honesty, emotional safety, transparency, "do I believe you'll show up?")
- Lessons: How trust is built in small moments · Kinds of betrayal (not only affairs) · What real accountability looks like · Rebuilding takes consistency, not grand gestures · The partner who broke trust: shame vs. guilt
- Exercises: Trust timeline · "Promises I can actually keep" commitment builder · Transparency agreements · Weekly reliability log
- Conversation guides: Accountability conversation (structured apology: acknowledge, impact, ownership, repair plan) · "What I need to feel safe" · Monthly trust check-in

**5. Healing After Infidelity**: a separate, gated module with different handling
- Gating: both partners confirm the affair has ended and contact has stopped (or there is a plan to end it). The app recommends a therapist up front.
- Assessment: *Affair Recovery Stage* (crisis/atone, attune, attach), betrayed partner's trauma symptoms, and the involved partner's readiness
- Lessons: Why you feel like this (betrayal trauma, triggers, intrusive thoughts) · The three phases of recovery · Questions that help vs. questions that hurt · For the involved partner: holding steady through anger · Understanding vulnerabilities without excusing the choice · When to see a therapist
- Exercises: Trigger plan · Transparency agreement (devices, schedule) · Separate tracks: "For the hurt partner" and "For the partner who strayed" · Writing an impact letter (private draft → optional share)
- Conversation guides: Structured Q&A session (time-boxed, with rules) · Responding to a trigger in real time · Building our new relationship ("Relationship 2.0")
- Safety rule: an affair is never disclosed through the app. Content is shown only after both partners self-identify into the module.

**6. Forgiveness & Releasing Resentment**
- Assessment: *Resentment Load* (lingering hurts, keeping score, readiness to forgive)
- Lessons: What forgiveness is and isn't · Resentment as an unmet need · Forgiveness as a process, not an event · Self-forgiveness
- Exercises: Inventory of old hurts (private) · Unsent letter · Separating "the hurt" from "the story" · Choosing which hurts to bring forward
- Conversation guides: Bringing up an old hurt safely · Offering and receiving amends
- Ritual: Symbolic "letting go" closure activity

### Tier C: Reconnect
**7. Deepening Connection (Emotional Intimacy)**
- Assessment: *Connection Check* (feeling known, emotional responsiveness, loneliness in the relationship, attachment style indicators)
- Lessons: Bids for connection (turning toward, away, against) · Attachment styles in couples · Softening: showing the softer feeling under the anger · Love maps: staying curious · Emotional accessibility and engagement
- Exercises: Bids tracker (log a day of bids) · Love map quiz ("How well do you know me now?") · Identifying my softer emotions · Attachment-needs cards
- Conversation guides: "Hold me tight" style reaching out · 36-questions-style deepening questions (in original wording) · Stress-reducing conversation (daily)
- Ritual: 14-day "6 seconds and 20 minutes" (a long kiss and daily debrief)

**8. Exploring Intimacy (Sex & Physical Affection)**
- Assessment: *Intimacy Profile* (satisfaction, desire discrepancy, comfort talking about sex, affection outside sex, pain or health factors); 18+ confirmation required
- Lessons: Desire discrepancy is normal · Spontaneous vs. responsive desire · Accelerators and brakes · Rebuilding physical affection after distance or betrayal · When to see a doctor or sex therapist
- Exercises: Yes/No/Maybe list. Each partner fills it privately and **only mutual matches are revealed**. · Accelerators and brakes worksheet · Non-sexual touch menu · A "sensate focus" style graduated touch plan (no-pressure stages)
- Conversation guides: Talking about sex without blame · Initiation and declining gracefully · Planning intimacy together
- Ritual: 7-day affection-only challenge (no pressure)

**9. Rebuilding Friendship & Fun**
- Assessment: *Friendship & Positivity* (fondness, admiration, shared fun, positive vs. negative ratio)
- Lessons: Why positivity outweighs negativity · Fondness and admiration as a buffer · Novelty and shared adventures · Playfulness heals
- Exercises: "What I first loved about you" · Admiration deck · Bucket list builder · Date planner (budget and energy filters)
- Conversation guides: Our story (how we met, revisited) · Appreciation exchange
- Ritual: 4-week weekly date challenge

**10. Shared Meaning & Future Vision**
- Assessment: *Shared Meaning* (values alignment, rituals, roles, goals)
- Lessons: Why couples need a shared "why" · Rituals of connection · Roles: what partner, parent and provider mean to each of us · Growing apart vs. growing together
- Exercises: Values card sort (compare results) · Design our rituals (mornings, reunions, holidays) · Five-year vision board
- Conversation guides: Dreams and life goals · Creating a relationship mission statement

### Tier D: Specific stressors
**11. Money as a Team**
- Assessment: *Money Harmony* (money histories, spender/saver styles, secrecy, decision-making)
- Lessons: Money is never just money · Your money story · Financial infidelity · Systems: shared, separate or hybrid
- Exercises: Money autobiography · Full disclosure worksheet · Budget-style picker · Shared goals planner
- Conversation guides: Money date (monthly) · Resolving a spending conflict

**12. Partners in Parenting & Household Load**
- Assessment: *Fair Share* (division of chores, mental load, parenting alignment, feeling like a team)
- Lessons: The invisible mental load · Couple vs. parent identity · Unified front, different styles · Protecting the couple from kid stress
- Exercises: Household task audit (who conceives, plans and executes each task) · Rebalance plan · Parenting values comparison
- Conversation guides: Redistributing the load · Disagreeing about discipline · Weekly family logistics meeting

**13. Family, In-Laws & Boundaries**
- Assessment: *Boundaries & Loyalty* (in-law involvement, feeling prioritized, friends, outside influences, technology and phones)
- Lessons: Leaving and cleaving: becoming a "we" · Healthy vs. rigid boundaries · Loyalty binds · Phones, social media and attention
- Exercises: Boundary map · "Our team first" agreements · Holiday and visit planner · Tech agreement
- Conversation guides: Discussing a family member without attacking · Setting a boundary together

**14. At a Crossroads (Deciding Together)**: discernment for couples where one or both are unsure whether to stay
- Assessment: *Commitment & Ambivalence* (leaning in vs. leaning out, mixed agenda, what would have to change)
- Lessons: Mixed-agenda couples are common · Three paths: status quo, separation, or an all-in 6-month effort · Your own part in the problem · What "all-in" really means
- Exercises: Private ambivalence journal (never shared) · My contributions inventory · Decision clarity worksheet
- Conversation guides: Sharing where I am, honestly · Agreeing on a path and a timeframe
- Off-ramps: amicable separation resources and therapist referral. The app never pushes a couple to stay together.

**Optional v2 modules:** Navigating Big Life Transitions (new baby, illness, job loss, grief); Addiction & Recovery in Relationships; Mental Health in the Relationship (supporting a partner with depression or anxiety); Rebuilding After Separation.

## Part 3: Global (non-module) content
- **Onboarding Relationship Health Check.** About 40 items: a short screen for each module domain plus a satisfaction measure. Use original items, or the free **CSI-4/CSI-16 Couples Satisfaction Index**, after confirming its licence. Do not copy the Gottman, DAS or PREPARE/ENRICH instruments. The result is a ranked **recommended path**, for example Calming the Storm → Communicating → Repairing Trust.
- **Private safety screen.** Each partner answers alone. It uses validated-style items covering fear, coercive control, emotional abuse, and physical or sexual violence (adapted from HITS/WAST or the Danger Assessment, with permission). The result is **never shared** with the partner and does not appear in the partner's view in any form.
- **Daily pulse** and **weekly State of Us** check-ins, with trend charts.
- **"Us Score" / progress dashboard.** Shows module progress, assessment retakes over time, streaks and milestones.
- **Library search** across all modules, filtered by type, time, solo/together, and intensity (light/medium/deep).
- **Help & Resources.** Therapist finder links, crisis lines (US: 988; National Domestic Violence Hotline 1-800-799-7233 / text START to 88788), and an "Is the app enough?" guidance page.

## Part 4: Safety & privacy system (cross-cutting, required before launch)
1. A **safety screen positive** result triggers a *safety mode* for that user. It hides together-activities and conflict-raising content, shows safety-planning resources and a quick-exit button, and makes the app look normal to the partner. It never notifies the partner.
2. **Privacy levels per item:** `private` (default for journals and safety), `shared_summary`, `shared_full`. Assessment comparisons need both partners to opt in.
3. **Quick exit plus app lock** (Face ID or PIN) and a discreet app icon option.
4. **Crisis keyword detection** in free text, for self-harm or violence, surfaces resources.
5. **Clinical disclaimer.** The app is not therapy. A "talk to a professional" recommendation appears when assessment scores pass severity thresholds.
6. **Clinical advisory review.** A licensed couples therapist (LMFT) reviews all content before launch. This is a budgeted step, not optional.
7. Data protection: Supabase RLS, encrypted at rest, account deletion and export, and unlinking that cleanly separates shared data.
